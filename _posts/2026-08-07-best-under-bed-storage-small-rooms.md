## How to buy under-bed storage without wasting money

Measure three things before you order anything: the clearance from the floor to
the underside of the bed frame, the distance from the floor to the lowest part
of the side rail (often lower than the frame, and the thing that actually blocks
a box), and how far under you can reach without lying down. Those three numbers
rule out most of what's for sale.

Then buy in this order — wheels first, soft boxes second, vacuum bags third.
Almost everybody buys a set of matching hard bins first and then discovers that
the back two are unreachable, so they store the things nobody wants and the room
is no tidier than it was.

## Common questions

### What is the best under-bed storage for a small room?

A [rolling drawer with wheels](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=under+bed+rolling+storage+drawer+with+wheels&tag=tamirugc20-20),
as long as your clearance allows it. In a small room the whole point is to use
the deep back half of the space, and wheels are the only feature that makes that
half genuinely reachable. If clearance is tight, a zip-top fabric box is the
next best thing.

### How much clearance do I need under a bed for storage?

Most rigid boxes need about 15cm; soft zip-top bags start working from about
10cm. Measure to the lowest obstruction, not the frame — side rails and centre
supports usually hang below it. If you're short by a few centimetres,
[bed risers](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bed+risers+heavy+duty&tag=tamirugc20-20)
are far cheaper than a different bed.

### Is it bad to store things under your bed?

Only if you store them badly. Keep it off the floor where you can, use lidded or
zipped containers so dust doesn't settle on fabric, and leave the middle strip
clear so the floor can be cleaned. Damp is the real enemy — use breathable bags
for clothes rather than sealed plastic.

### Can you use vacuum bags for duvets long-term?

For synthetic duvets and coats, yes, and they're the best space saver there is.
For down, feather and wool, unpack them once a year and let them loft again —
long compression can leave permanent flat spots. A hand pump is more reliable
than a vacuum-hose fitting.
