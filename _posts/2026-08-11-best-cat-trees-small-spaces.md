A cat in a small flat is not short of space. It is short of **height**. Cats
want to sit above the room and watch it, and when there is nowhere legitimate to
do that they pick the bookshelf, the fridge, or the top of your wardrobe.

So the useful question is not "which cat tree is biggest" — it is which one buys
the most vertical space per square foot of floor. We compared the trees, perches
and shelves that work in a small home, sorted by the problem each one solves.
Measure the floor patch you can genuinely give up first, and the window sill
while you're there. Those two numbers rule out most of what's sold.

## 1. A tall, narrow cat tree — the best first buy in a small room

Most cat trees are sold on how many platforms they have. In a small home the
number that matters is the base. A
[tall narrow cat tree](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=tall+narrow+cat+tree+small+spaces&tag=tamirugc20-20)
with a footprint around 40–50 cm square gives a cat three or four levels out of a
patch of floor you were only using to walk around anyway.

Two things to check before buying: the base must be heavier than it looks, or a
cat landing on the top platform will rock it, and at least one platform should be
a covered box rather than a flat pad. Cats alternate between wanting to see and
wanting to hide, and a tree offering only one of those gets used half as much.

## 2. A window perch — the cheapest way to buy a cat a view

If you have no floor to give, give a window. A
[suction-cup window perch](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=cat+window+perch+suction+cup&tag=tamirugc20-20)
mounts straight onto the glass, holds a surprising amount of cat, and costs a
fraction of a tree. It is also the single most-used object in most of the small
homes we looked at, because it combines the two things cats rank highest: a
warm spot and moving things to watch.

Clean the glass with rubbing alcohol before you mount it — suction cups fail on
film, not on weight — and re-press the cups every couple of months. Pick one
rated well above your cat's weight, and put it on a window that catches morning
sun rather than the brightest one.

## 3. Wall-mounted cat shelves — a climbing route that costs no floor at all

A blank wall above head height is the most under-used space in a small flat.
[Wall-mounted cat shelves](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wall+mounted+cat+shelves+climbing&tag=tamirugc20-20)
turn it into a route: three or four steps rising to a wider platform, ending
somewhere the cat can sit and look down at the room.

Space the steps about 30–40 cm apart vertically, stagger them so the climb is a
zigzag rather than a ladder, and screw into studs — this is the one item here
where the fixing matters more than the product. Carpeted tops beat bare wood; a
cat that slips once will not use the route again.

## 5. A tall sisal scratching post — the one that saves your sofa

A short scratching post is the most common wasted purchase in cat ownership.
Cats scratch to stretch, so the post has to be taller than the cat is long when
fully extended — for most adults that means at least 70 cm. A
[tall sisal scratching post](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=tall+sisal+cat+scratching+post+heavy+base&tag=tamirugc20-20)
with a wide, weighted base is the cheapest piece of furniture protection you can
buy, and it pays for itself the first time it is chosen over an armchair.

## How to choose, in one paragraph

If you have any floor to spare, buy the narrow tree first — height is what your
cat is short of, and nothing else delivers as much of it. If you truly have none,
buy the window perch, which costs less and gets used more than most people
expect. Add a tall scratching post either way, because it is the item that stops
your furniture becoming the alternative. Everything else on this page is a
refinement of those three.

## Common questions

### What are the best cat corner ideas for a small apartment?

Build upward in one corner rather than spreading things around the room. A tall
narrow tree against the wall, a
[window perch](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=cat+window+perch+suction+cup&tag=tamirugc20-20)
on the nearest window and a covered bed underneath gives a cat height, a view and
a hiding place in about half a square metre. Our
[cozy cat corner ideas](/cozy-cat-corner-ideas/) walk through the layout.

### Do cat window perches actually hold a full-grown cat?

Yes, when they are mounted properly. Most suction models are rated well beyond
average cat weight; the failures come from dirty glass, not from the load. Clean
the pane with rubbing alcohol, press each cup firmly, and re-seat them every
couple of months. Choose a rating with plenty of headroom over your cat's weight.

### Is a cat tree or wall shelves better in a small room?

Shelves if you own the place and have a blank wall — they use no floor at all. A
tree if you rent, because nothing is drilled and it moves with you. Shelves are
also the better answer with two cats, since a climbing route lets them pass each
other without a confrontation on a single platform.

### How tall should a scratching post be?

Taller than your cat is long with its front legs stretched above its head, which
for most adult cats means 70 cm or more. Height and a heavy base matter far more
than material or looks: a post that wobbles gets abandoned immediately, and a
short one never gets used at all.
