Open shelving is the cheapest storage a small home can get, and it is also the
easiest thing in the house to get wrong. Shelves hold everything you own in full
view, so the room reads as clutter even when it is perfectly organised.

A basket fixes that in one move: it turns twelve unrelated objects into one calm
rectangle. But baskets are sold on looks, and looks are the least useful spec.
What decides whether a basket earns its shelf is whether it **fits the opening**,
whether it **holds its shape when it is half empty**, and whether you can tell
what is inside without pulling it out.

So measure two things before you buy anything: the width and the depth of the
shelf opening, and the height of the gap above it. Almost every returned basket
we have ever heard about failed on one of those three numbers.

## 4. Clear stackable bins — when you need to see, not hide

Woven baskets hide things beautifully, which is a problem for anything you have
to find quickly.
[Clear stackable bins](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=clear+stackable+storage+bins+with+lids&tag=tamirugc20-20)
belong in the places nobody sees — inside a wardrobe, on a utility shelf, under
the stairs — where knowing what is in the box beats how the box looks.

Stackable is the operative word. A bin with a flat, recessed lid holds another
bin steady on top; a domed lid wastes the entire gap above it.

## 5. A rolling under-shelf basket — free storage you already own

The gap between one shelf and the things sitting on it is dead space in almost
every kitchen and bathroom. An
[under-shelf basket](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=under+shelf+basket+organizer&tag=tamirugc20-20)
clips over the shelf board and hangs into that gap, adding a second layer without
any drilling and without costing a millimetre of floor.

They come in two clip widths. Measure the thickness of your shelf board first —
a wire shelf and a 25 mm chipboard shelf need different clips, and the wrong one
simply falls off.

## Common questions

### What size storage baskets fit a standard shelf?

Measure the opening, then subtract about 2 cm from the width and the depth so
the basket slides rather than wedges. Standard cube shelving takes an 11-inch
(28 cm) cube, and most bookcase shelves take a basket 30–33 cm deep. Height is
the number people forget — leave a couple of centimetres of clearance so you can
lift it out without tipping it.

### Are fabric bins or woven baskets better?

Woven for anywhere on show, fabric for anywhere behind a door. Woven natural
fibre holds its shape when it is half empty and looks like part of the room;
[fabric cubes](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=fabric+storage+cubes+11+inch&tag=tamirugc20-20)
are cheaper, lighter and fine inside a wardrobe or a cube unit where nobody
judges them.

### How do you organise open shelving without it looking cluttered?

Repeat one basket and leave gaps. Identical containers in a row read as a single
calm band, and roughly a third of the shelf left empty is what makes the rest
look chosen rather than crammed. Our
[14 small-space storage ideas](/small-space-storage-ideas/) work through the same
principle room by room.

### What can I use instead of buying baskets?

Shoe boxes covered in plain paper, sturdy gift boxes and old drawer inserts all
work, as long as they match each other — the uniformity is doing the work, not
the price. Start with the shelf that annoys you most, live with it for a week,
then buy the real thing only for the sizes that proved useful.
